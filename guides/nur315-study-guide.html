Abdomen & GI

Week 7 · Jarvis Ch. 22

Abdomen Internal anatomy & viscera

  • All organs inside the abdominal cavity are called viscera.
  • The peritoneum lines the wall (parietal) and covers most organs (visceral).
  • Solid viscera keep a fixed shape: liver, pancreas, spleen, adrenal glands, kidneys, ovaries, uterus. They should not swell and shrink with meals.
  • Hollow viscera change shape with contents: stomach, gallbladder, small intestine, colon, bladder.
  • Midline organs: aorta, the uterus if enlarged, the bladder if distended.
MnemonicSolid = SPLAKOU (Spleen, Pancreas, Liver, Adrenals, Kidneys, Ovaries, Uterus). If it's not on that list, it's hollow.

Abdomen Four quadrants — what lives where ⭐

RUQLUQ
Liver, gallbladder, duodenum, head of pancreas, right kidney (upper), right adrenal, hepatic flexure Left lobe of liver, stomach, spleen, body of pancreas, left kidney (upper), left adrenal, splenic flexure
RLQLLQ
Cecum, appendix, right ovary/tube, right ureter, ascending colon, right kidney (lower) Sigmoid colon, descending colon, left ovary/tube, left ureter, left kidney (lower)
  • Right/left are the patient's right and left, so the image looks reversed to you.
  • Nine-region system (from two midclavicular + two horizontal planes): hypochondriac (below ribs), epigastric (above stomach), lumbar (low back), umbilical, iliac (iliac crest), hypogastric (below stomach). Four-quadrant is used more in practice; both show up on ATI/NCLEX.

Abdomen Exam sequence — the big rule ⭐

  • Abdomen breaks the usual order: Inspect → Auscultate → Percuss → Palpate.
  • Auscultate before touching so you don't stir up false bowel sounds.
  • Prep: supine and flat, empty bladder first, warm the stethoscope, expose only the abdomen, keep the patient relaxed, assess painful areas last.
Mnemonic"I Always Percuss Patients" = Inspect, Auscultate, Percuss, Palpate. (Everywhere else it's IPPA — the abdomen is the exception.)

Abdomen Marpole's required quadrant order ⭐

  • Use RLQ → RUQ → LUQ → LLQ for bowel sounds, percussion, and light palpation — even if a demo video shows another order.
  • Start in the RLQ (ileocecal valve): sounds are almost always present there.
Mnemonic"Run Right Up, then Left Low." Begin low-right, go up the right, across to upper-left, down to lower-left.

Abdomen Inspection

  • Contour: flat, rounded, scaphoid, or protuberant/distended (view from the foot of the bed).
  • Umbilicus: normally midline and inverted; note discoloration, drainage, inflammation, or herniation.
  • Skin: color, scars, surgical scars, lesions, striae; healed vs. draining piercings.
  • Normal aortic pulsation may show in the epigastric area in thin, relaxed people.
  • Pubic hair: diamond shape in males, inverted triangle in females.

Abdomen 7 F's of distension ⭐

Mnemonic — 7 F'sFat · Fluid · Flatus · Feces · Fetus · Fibroids · Fatal growth (tumor).
  • Obesity → tympany with scattered dullness.
  • Ascites → tympany on top, dullness in the dependent sides where fluid pools; dullness shifts when the patient rolls; a fluid wave supports ascites.

Abdomen Auscultation — bowel sounds ⭐

  • Use the diaphragm; high-pitched, gurgling, irregular sounds.
  • Marpole's frequency categories: normoactive ≈ 5–30/min, hypoactive < 5/min, hyperactive > 30/min. (Some texts say 5–34; follow the course convention on tests.)
  • Do not chart "absent" until you've listened a full 5 minutes — absent sounds can be an emergency.
  • Borborygmi = loud rumbling/growling (hyperperistalsis).
  • Hypoactive can follow surgery or inflammation; hyperactive signals increased motility.

Abdomen Auscultation — vascular sounds & bruits ⭐

  • Aorta: midline, just below the xiphoid (between xiphoid and umbilicus).
  • Renal arteries: lateral and slightly below the aorta. Iliac: ~1 inch below and 1 inch lateral to umbilicus. Femoral: over the femoral pulses.
  • Arteries sit deep — use slightly firmer pressure with the diaphragm.
  • A bruit = blowing/turbulent sound from narrowing (plaque, stenosis, aneurysm) → report it.
Do not do thisNever palpate the aorta after hearing a bruit or when an aneurysm is suspected — pressure can trigger rupture.

Abdomen Percussion

  • Percuss all four quadrants in Marpole's order.
  • Tympany over air-filled bowel; dullness over solid organs, fluid, stool, or a mass.
  • One point per quadrant is enough for the abbreviated checkoff; the zigzag pattern is the fuller technique.

Abdomen Palpation — light & deep

  • Light first (finger pads, gentle rotary motion), then deep. Patient stays flat.
  • Watch for guarding, rigidity, facial grimacing; palpate a painful area last.
  • Normally palpable: liver edge, aorta, some intestines, a full bladder; mild tenderness over the sigmoid is normal.
  • Document a mass by: location, size, shape, consistency, surface, mobility, pulsatility, tenderness.
  • Normal charting: "soft, nontender abdomen without masses."

Abdomen Organ palpation landmarks ⭐

  • Liver: may bump your fingers just below the right costal margin on inspiration; a firm, regular ridge. Should not extend far below the ribs.
  • Spleen: normally not palpable — must be ~3× enlarged to feel. If you feel it, stop — it's friable and can rupture. Enlarged in mono, leukemia/lymphoma, portal HTN, HIV.
  • Kidneys: right may occasionally be palpable; left usually is not (it sits ~1 cm higher).
  • Aorta: normally 2.5–4 cm wide, pulsates anteriorly. Widened = possible AAA (pulsating mass left of midline, ± bruit).

Abdomen CVA tenderness (kidneys) ⭐

  • Find the angle between the spine and the 12th rib posteriorly.
  • Lay one hand flat over it; strike with the ulnar edge / closed fist of the other (indirect fist percussion) — tissues vibrate rather than make sound.
  • Normal = a thud, no pain. Pain = kidney inflammation/irritation (infection, stones, chronic disease). Assess both sides and document.
  • Add this while you're already behind the patient during the posterior thoracic/lung exam.

Abdomen Appendicitis & special tests ⭐

Mnemonic"My Patient Órdered Rebound Rovsing" → McBurney, Psoas, Obturator, Rebound, Rovsing.
  • McBurney's point (memorize this one): RLQ, ~⅓ of the way from the anterior superior iliac spine toward the umbilicus. Tenderness → inflamed appendix.
  • Psoas sign: extend the right hip/leg backward → pain near an inflamed appendix.
  • Obturator sign: flex the right hip & knee, then internally rotate → pelvic/appendiceal irritation.
  • Rovsing's sign: press-and-release the LLQ → pain felt in the RLQ is positive. (Recording said "Roth" — the real name is Rovsing.)
  • Rebound tenderness: press slowly, release quickly → more pain on release = peritoneal irritation. Do it gently and only once.

Abdomen Hernias, ascites & aging

  • Hernia: fat or intestine pushes through a weak spot in the abdominal wall (umbilical, epigastric, incisional, inguinal); may need surgery.
  • Aging: relaxed wall muscle, delayed gastric emptying, less gastric acid, more gallstones, impaired drug metabolism, frequent constipation.
  • Constipation drivers: low activity, low fluid, low fiber, meds (narcotics), IBS, obstruction, hypothyroidism, poor toileting access.

Abdomen Bristol Stool Scale ⭐

Mnemonic"1–2 hard as stone · 3–4–5 in the zone · 6–7 run for the throne." Goal is types 3–5.
  • Types 1–2: hard → too much water absorbed → constipation/dehydration.
  • Types 6–7: diarrhea → fluid & electrolyte loss risk.
  • The RN assesses/documents stool (an assessment task); the aide may only report that a BM happened. Chart by Bristol number or descriptor per the EHR.

Rectum, Anus & Prostate

Week 8 · Jarvis Ch. 26

Rectum Structure

  • The anal canal is the GI outlet, lined with modified skin (no hair/sebaceous glands), with mucus glands and internal + external sphincters.
  • Anal columns (columns of Morgagni) each hold an artery and vein.
  • The rectum is the distal large intestine; the sigmoid connects to it and it ends at the anal canal. Interior has three valves of Houston.

Rectum Nursing scope & the DRE ⭐

  • Nurses inspect the external area and may insert a rectal tube — but do not perform a digital rectal exam with their own finger.
  • A DRE assesses the prostate for: size, shape, surface, consistency, mobility, sensitivity. Positions: left lateral, standing/bending, or lithotomy.
  • In trauma: rectal tone helps evaluate spinal cord injury; blood may signal internal injury.
  • Rectal tubes collect diarrhea, but prolonged pressure damages the rectum/sphincter and can cause incontinence.
Mnemonic — DRE findings"Some Shapes Sit Comfortably, Mostly Sensitive" = Size, Shape, Surface, Consistency, Mobility, Sensitivity.

Rectum Anorectal abnormalities

  • A fissure/fistula is a tract connecting the rectum to another structure or the outside; stool reaching the urinary tract can cause infection.
  • Hemorrhoids are internal or external — streaky red stool suggests hemorrhoidal bleeding; bright/dark red throughout suggests a GI bleed.
  • Others: pilonidal cyst/sinus, pruritus ani, fecal impaction, abscess, rectal polyp, carcinoma.

Rectum Prostate gland

  • Sits below the bladder and in front of the rectal wall; the urethra runs through it, so enlargement narrows the urethra and makes voiding harder.
  • Two seminal vesicles secrete fructose-rich fluid (nourishes sperm); Cowper's (bulbourethral) glands add clear mucus that neutralizes acidic urine.
  • Abnormalities: benign prostatic hypertrophy (BPH), prostatitis, carcinoma.

Reproductive & Urinary

Week 8 · Jarvis Ch. 25 (male) & 27 (female)

Male Anatomy

  • External: penis, scrotum. Internal: testes, epididymis, vas deferens. Accessory glands: prostate, seminal vesicles, bulbourethral.
  • The scrotum keeps testes below body temperature — sperm develop best when cooler; the cremaster muscle adjusts scrotal size to temperature.
  • Left testis hangs lower (longer left spermatic cord).
  • The epididymis stores sperm and links testis to the ducts; epididymitis is an infection that can be sexually transmitted.
  • The male urethra serves both urinary and reproductive functions.

Male Testicular self-exam (TSE) ⭐

Mnemonic — TSET = Timing (monthly) · S = Shower (warm water relaxes the sac) · E = Examine & report changes.
  • Teach every male from ~13–14 through adulthood; roll each testis gently to find masses.
  • Early detection improves when he knows his own normal consistency.

Female Anatomy & UTI risk

  • External: mons pubis, labia majora/minora, clitoris, vestibule, urethral meatus, Skene's glands, vaginal orifice, Bartholin's glands.
  • Internal: vagina, cervix, uterus, fallopian tubes, fornices.
  • The female urethra is shorter than the male's → higher UTI risk.
  • The cervix guards the vaginal canal from the uterus.
  • The uterus supports fetal development; an ectopic pregnancy implants outside the uterus and generally can't survive.

Female Pap smear & pelvic exam ⭐

  • A Pap collects cervical cells to screen for cervical cancer — not endometrial or ovarian, and not vaginal-canal cells.
  • Don't collect during menses or heavy infectious discharge; patient should avoid douching, intercourse, or inserting anything for 24 h before; obtain the Pap before other specimens.
  • Lithotomy position gives access to the vaginal canal and rectum for exam/delivery.
  • Nurses don't assess the cervix directly. When a male clinician does a pelvic exam, a female staff member must be present and documented.

Female Menstrual history & prevention levels ⭐

  • Ask: age at first period, LMP, frequency/cycle/duration, flow (pads/tampons), clotting, pain & impact on ADLs, spotting, associated symptoms — because blood loss raises anemia risk.
  • Screening (like a Pap) is usually secondary prevention; screening someone who already has the disease can be tertiary.
Mnemonic — prevention1° = Prevent · 2° = Screen/detect early · 3° = Manage existing disease.

Urinary Urinalysis & urine color

  • Urinalysis can check specific gravity, glucose, ketones, protein, blood, WBCs, nitrates, electrolytes, drugs.
  • Creatinine is a stable marker of kidney function (muscle-metabolism end product); BUN (protein-metabolism end product) varies with many factors.
  • Dark urine usually = more solutes, but bright yellow can just be vitamins or energy drinks — not always dehydration. Color also shifts with meds, foods, contrast dye.

Breasts, Axillae & Lymphatics

Weeks 7 & 8 · Jarvis Ch. 18

Breast Anatomy & lymphatics ⭐

  • The tail of Spence (upper outer quadrant, toward the axilla) is the most common tumor site because of its lymphatic tissue.
  • Four axillary node groups: central, pectoral (anterior), subscapular (posterior), lateral → drain up to infraclavicular and supraclavicular nodes.
  • Breast cancer occurs in men and women.
Mnemonic"Spence is tense" — the upper-outer tail of Spence catches most tumors.

Breast Inspection findings

  • Assess size, shape, symmetry, skin, nipple position/discharge, retraction, dimpling, edema, fixation, masses, axillary nodes.
  • Slight asymmetry is normal; it's concerning when paired with fixation, dimpling, retraction, or a new change.
  • Nipples usually protrude; some are flat or inverted (normal if long-standing and can be everted). Note scaling, fissures, ulceration, bleeding, discharge. Supernumerary nipple is a normal variant.

Breast Palpation technique

  • Cover the whole breast and axilla with the finger pads, using light, medium, and deep pressure.
  • Patterns: vertical strip (preferred), spokes-on-a-wheel, or concentric circles.
  • If she found a lump herself, examine the unaffected breast first to learn her normal.
  • Describe a lump by: location (clock face + cm from nipple), size (3 dimensions), shape, consistency, mobility, distinctness, nipple changes, overlying skin, tenderness, lymphadenopathy.
  • A new lump always needs physician evaluation — even if painless or seemingly hormonal.

Breast Self-exam (BSE) teaching

  • Still taught, though mammography/ultrasound have made it less effective as early screening.
  • Monthly, at the same point in the cycle; postmenopausal patients pick the same day each month.
  • Teach in front of a mirror, then in the shower (soap aids gliding), then supine.

Breast Abnormal signs of retraction/inflammation ⭐

  • Dimpling, nipple retraction, edema (peau d'orange — orange-peel skin), fixation, irregular masses → all need evaluation.
  • Gynecomastia = benign male breast-tissue enlargement (a smooth, firm, movable disk) from hormonal imbalance; also seen with anabolic steroids, some meds, cirrhosis. Normal & temporary in puberty.
  • Nipple discharge in a non-pregnant woman or in a man may be pathologic.
Mnemonic — red flags"DENR" = Dimpling, Edema (peau d'orange), Nipple retraction, Retraction/fixation.

Breast Post-mastectomy precautions ⭐

Nothing on that armAfter a unilateral mastectomy, avoid BP cuffs, tourniquets, and IV/needle sticks on the affected side — impaired lymph drainage worsens edema. (Guidance may differ after newer or bilateral procedures.)

Mental Status Assessment

Week 10 · Jarvis Ch. 5

Mental What it is & the ABCT frame ⭐

  • Mental status = a person's emotional and cognitive functioning; it can't be measured directly like heart sounds — you infer it from behavior.
  • Four headings: Appearance, Behavior, Cognition, Thought process (A-B-C-T).
Mnemonic — ABCTAppearance (posture, dress, grooming/hygiene) · Behavior (LOC, facial expression, speech, mood/affect) · Cognition (orientation, attention, memory) · Thought process & content.

Mental Cognition & orientation

  • Orientation ×4: time, place, person, situation.
  • Recent memory = 24-hour recall; remote memory = long-term (e.g., first job).
  • Watch attention span and flight of ideas.

Mental Four Unrelated Words Test

  • A sensitive, valid memory test that avoids unverifiable recall.
  • Pick 4 words with different sounds/meanings; have the patient repeat them to confirm understanding.
  • Ask for recall at 5, 10, and 30 minutes. Normal under age 60 = 3–4 words at each interval.

Mental Aphasia

  • Loss of the ability to speak/write coherently or understand language, often after a CVA (stroke).
  • Test comprehension (name objects), reading (mind education level), and writing (a made-up sentence — note coherence, spelling, parts of speech).

Mental Anxiety & depression screens ⭐

  • GAD-7: 7-item anxiety scale; higher = greater likelihood; first 2 items are core anxiety; ≥3 suggests diagnosis.
  • PHQ-2: 2 questions (depressed mood + anhedonia) → gateway to the full PHQ-9 (9 items, higher = more impairment).
  • Anxiety and depression are the two most common mental-health disorders in patients seeking care.
Mnemonic — match the toolGAD = "Got Anxiety? 7." · PHQ-2 opens the door, PHQ-9 tells you more.

Mental Suicide screening ⭐

  • Screen if the person voices sadness, hopelessness, despair, or grief.
  • Start with general questions and go deeper on affirmative answers.
  • The risk of skipping the question is far greater than asking — you may be the only professional to catch the cue. Share concerns with a mental-health professional.
NoteThis section covers screening as taught in class. If any of this is affecting you personally, it's worth reaching out to someone you trust or a professional — say the word and I can point you to support resources.

Substance Use

Week 10 · Jarvis Ch. 6

Substance Alcohol & drug basics

  • Alcohol has dose-related effects — more drinking, more risk; many meds are alcohol-interactive.
  • Dependence raises risk of ED visits, ICU admissions, and sepsis; binge drinking adds risk.
  • Illicit categories: marijuana/hashish, cocaine, heroin, hallucinogens, inhalants, prescription drugs used non-medically.
  • Drug overdose deaths now exceed motor-vehicle deaths; opioid crisis drivers include more pain prescriptions, marketing, and downplayed addiction risk.
  • Diagnosis gold standard = DSM-5; alcohol problems are widely underdiagnosed.

Substance TWEAK questions ⭐

Mnemonic — TWEAKTolerance (how many drinks?) · Worry (have others complained?) · Eye-opener (morning drink?) · Amnesia (blackouts?) · Kut down (feel the need to?).

Substance CIWA (alcohol withdrawal) ⭐

  • Most sensitive objective withdrawal scale; 10 measured criteria + a composite score (includes vital signs and O₂ sat).
  • Score 0–7 → monitor every 4 hours. All scores under 8 for 72 hours → can discontinue.
  • Trended scores guide the level of monitoring.

Violence & Abuse

Week 10 · Jarvis Ch. 7

Violence Mandatory reporting

  • Nurses are mandatory reporters; know your state's laws and reporting requirements.
  • A SANE nurse (Sexual Assault Nurse Examiner) is specially trained to care for sexual-assault survivors.

Violence Intimate partner violence (IPV) — 4 categories ⭐

Mnemonic — IPV types"PSSP" = Physical · Sexual · Stalking · Psychological aggression. (Includes teen dating violence; sexting/cyber abuse can be a route.)

Violence IPV assessment & screening tools ⭐

  • Use open-ended questions; interview the person separately from the possible perpetrator.
  • Listen for cues or stories that don't match the injury.
  • Screens: a single question ("Do you feel safe at home?"), or standardized tools HITS (4 items) and STaT (3 items) — USPSTF prefers standardized tools.

Violence Child abuse & neglect (CAPTA)

  • Defined by the Child Abuse Prevention and Treatment Act (now includes sex/human trafficking and protections for infants with Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorder).
  • Four types: Neglect, Physical, Sexual, Emotional.
Mnemonic — child abuse types"NPSE" = Neglect · Physical · Sexual · Emotional.

Violence Older-adult abuse ⭐

  • Underreported; about 60% is by a family member.
  • Five forms: Physical, Sexual, Psychological/Emotional, Neglect, Financial exploitation.
Mnemonic — elder abuse"PSPNF" = Physical · Sexual · Psychological · Neglect · Financial.

Violence Objective data & health effects

  • Full-body visual exam — abuse may hide under clothing; look for bruises in different stages of healing, patterns shaped like objects, or significant injury in a non-mobile person.
  • Use consistent, accurate terminology; baseline labs: CBC w/ platelets, basic chemistries, LFTs, coagulation panel, UA.
  • Health effects: gynecologic/obstetric harm (preterm, low birth weight, perinatal death), depression, suicide, PTSD, substance use; abused children face lasting health and developmental impacts.

Nutritional Assessment

Week 10 · Jarvis Ch. 12

Nutrition Nutritional status ⭐

  • Nutritional status = balance between nutrient intake and requirements.
  • Optimal: intake meets daily needs + extra demands (growth, pregnancy, illness).
  • Under-nutrition: reserves depleted / intake too low.
  • Over-nutrition: excess calories, sodium, fat.
  • Affected by physiologic, psychosocial, developmental, cultural, and economic factors.

Nutrition Screening vs. full assessment

  • Screening parameters: weight & weight history, risk conditions, diet info, routine labs.
  • Anyone flagged gets a full assessment: dietary history + clinical info, physical exam (clinical signs + anthropometrics), and labs.
  • Clinical deficiency signs show up first where tissue turns over fast: skin, hair, mouth, lips, eyes — but they're late signs, so labs confirm.

Nutrition Dietary intake tools

Mnemonic — the "R-F-D" recallsRecall (24-hour) · Frequency questionnaire · Diary/record (usually 3 days) · plus direct observation.
  • Guideline references: ChooseMyPlate, Dietary Guidelines, Dietary Reference Intakes (DRIs).

Nutrition Anthropometric measures ⭐

  • BMI: practical marker of weight-for-height; flags obesity or protein-calorie malnutrition.
  • Waist-to-hip ratio & waist circumference: body-fat distribution / health risk (WC alone predicts risk).
  • Percent usual body weight (formula); arm span when height is hard to measure.
  • Serial measures weekly/biweekly/monthly per population.
  • General-appearance clues: obese, cachectic (fat + muscle wasting), or edematous.

Cultural & Spiritual Assessment

Week 10 · Jarvis Ch. 2

Culture Core concepts & terms ⭐

  • Culture = shared knowledge, beliefs, customs, skills; learned, shared, adaptive, and ever-changing.
  • Ethnicity = social group with shared traits; ethnic identity = self-identifying with that group.
  • Acculturation = adopting the majority culture. Assimilation = one-directional, more forced — losing most of one's own. Biculturalism/integration = reciprocal change while keeping identity. Acculturative stress = the health toll of adapting.
MnemonicAcculturation = Add · Assimilation = Abandon · Biculturalism = Both.

Culture Health disparities & SDOH ⭐

  • Social Determinants of Health affect a person from preconception to death.
  • Evidence says poverty has the greatest influence on health status.
  • All racial/ethnic minority groups exceed the national poverty level; low education/income correlate with disability risk.

Culture Folk healers & competence levels

  • Cultures have their own healers (e.g., curandero, shaman/medicine woman, herbalist, braucher). Healing isn't complete unless body and mind are healed.
  • Progression: culturally sensitive (has knowledge) → appropriate (applies it to outcomes) → competent (applies it universally to all care contexts).
  • No single tool fits all — don't stereotype; listen and learn.
Mnemonic — competence ladderSensitive → Appropriate → Competent ("Sense it, Apply it, Master it").

Culture Spiritual assessment frameworks ⭐

  • Spirituality = belief in something larger than oneself; religion = an organized shared belief system. They overlap but don't have to co-exist.
FICAHOPE
Faith/belief · Importance · Community · Address in care Hope/meaning sources · Organized religion · Personal practice · Effects on care/end-of-life
MnemonicFICA for a quick faith check; HOPE when you need meaning + end-of-life; SPIRIT is the most detailed (adds rituals, restrictions, terminal planning).
